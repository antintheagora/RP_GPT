Credits

RP‑GPT runs on your own machine. It writes with a language model you host, draws with an image model you host, and asks the internet for nothing — not a typeface, not a script, not a line of prose.

Borrowed work

Two models in the painted hall are not ours. They are used under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence, which asks for exactly this: that the people who made them are named wherever their work appears.

The licence is at creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/, written out rather than linked. This game never asks the internet for anything, and a credits page is a poor place to make the first exception — naming the author, the licence and the source is what CC‑BY actually asks for.

Models it drives

These are separate programs on your machine, not part of the game. RP‑GPT talks to them and works without them — without a language model there is no prose, and without ComfyUI there are simply no pictures.

Gemma, through Ollama
writes the prose and plays every character. gemma4:12b narrates; gemma3:latest is the Keeper, who rates the fiction and never decides an outcome.
FLUX.2 Klein, through ComfyUI
draws the scenes, when it is running. flux-2-klein-4b-fp8 with a qwen_3_4b text encoder.

Made for this game

Every frame, button, backdrop and scene is modelled in Blender and rendered from source rather than drawn once and kept — so a change to the light is a change to the light, not a repaint. The rules, the dice, the clocks and the writing are the author's.
