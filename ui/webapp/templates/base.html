Keys
Esc
Open or close this menu. Inside a written action it cancels the box; with the character sheet open it closes the sheet first.
C
The character sheet. Not while you are writing an action -- there are Cs in that.
Space
Stop waiting. The narration is revealed at reading speed; this gives you the rest of it at once, for this turn only.
Tab
Move between controls. While a dialogue is open it stays inside it, so nothing behind can be reached by accident.
The words this game uses
Bearing
How well one approach suits one problem, from Ideal to Futile. The Keeper rates all seven at once and the rating sticks: the door is hard for the same reason on turn six as on turn one.
Position
How exposed you are if it goes wrong — Poised, Risky, Desperate. It never changes your odds. It bounds how bad the consequence may be, and it is always on screen.
Effect
How well it went, read off the face of the die rather than off the margin. That is what lets an improbable success feel like one.
Clock
A pressure you can count. Your progress fills as you get somewhere; closing in fills as the world does. Nothing ticks on a timer — something moved because you acted.
Tide
A force with a plan, moving while you are busy elsewhere. It takes its next step on its own schedule.
Resolve
Nerve. Spend it to push for better odds, or to refuse a consequence that has already landed. It runs out.
Fortune
One reroll for the whole campaign. Arm it before you act and you see the first die before deciding to use it.
The Bargain
Better odds at a stated price. You are buying the odds, not the outcome: the cost lands whether or not it works.
Affinity
What one person thinks of you. Reputation is what a faction has heard. They move separately.
Scars and Virtues
Permanent marks, bad and good. Four scars and the character retires.
